# Visualization rules

Use the simplest visual that preserves truth.

## Metric cards

Use for 3–6 high-value numbers that establish intuition. Every number needs unit/context and a source or scope label.

## Horizontal bars

Use for directly comparable values: price, battery, market share under one research definition, storage, shipment, or survey percentages.

Rules:

- same denominator / period / unit;
- bar length should encode actual ratio;
- do not imply statistical precision beyond the source;
- add a caveat under the chart.

## Comparison tables

Use when dimensions are mixed units or need explanatory “so what?”. The most important column is not the spec itself but **implication for positioning**.

Highlight target and competitor sparingly. Do not color every cell.

## Journey strip

Use 5–8 steps. Each step should contain the consumer action and a concise evidence need.

## Positioning/value map

Use a matrix for mental territory, current asset, competitor asset, what to strengthen, and what not to fight.

## Price ladder

Compare like-for-like SKUs. Always state memory/storage configuration. A cheaper entry SKU is not proof of lower price at every tier.

## Opportunity/risk cards

Use four quadrants/cards for:

- competitor strength;
- our asset;
- our risk;
- competitor vulnerability.

## GTM priority matrix

Use `P0 / P1 / P2 | strategy | operator action | KPI`. P0 must be small enough to execute now; do not make everything P0.

## A/B test cards

Each test should change one primary variable. Include primary outcome and guardrail where possible.

## No fake charts

Do not chart:

- unverified platform popularity;
- incompatible market-share studies;
- rumor-based specs;
- “scores” invented by the model;
- subjective wins unless the scoring rule is explicit and evidence-backed.
