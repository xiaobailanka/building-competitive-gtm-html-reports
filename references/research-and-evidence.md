# Research and evidence protocol

## Evidence ladder

Use the strongest available source for each claim.

### Grade A — primary / authoritative

- manufacturer official product/spec/press pages;
- regulator/government/statistics bureau;
- exchange filings / annual reports / investor relations;
- official marketplace brand flagship pages when price/benefit is the subject;
- established research firm original publication with methodology.

### Grade B — reputable secondary reporting

- major business/technology press reporting on a launch or first-party announcement;
- professional reviews with transparent test method;
- channel reporting that links to or quotes primary information.

### Grade C — directional signal only

- retailer promotion snapshots;
- analyst/social posts;
- third-party sell-through estimates;
- creator/community discussions;
- leaked material or rumor.

Grade C can guide a hypothesis but cannot anchor a core factual conclusion without corroboration.

## Evidence ledger

Before writing, maintain a table with:

`claim | metric/value | product/SKU | geography | date | source | grade | scope/method | caveat | use in report`

Every charted number must exist in the ledger.

## Fact-state labels

Use these internally and surface them when useful:

- **Verified fact** — directly supported by Grade A.
- **Sourced report** — supported by Grade B/C and attributed.
- **Analytical inference** — reasoned from verified facts; not a measured consumer fact.
- **Test hypothesis** — an operating proposition that must be validated.

## Freshness rules

For launches, pricing, SKU, availability, promotions, rankings and market share, verify current information on the analysis date. If a product is unreleased, say so explicitly and separate announced, reported, and rumored information.

## Comparability rules

Do not compare numbers unless the denominator and scope match. Examples:

- same country/region;
- same period;
- same model family or comparable SKU;
- same definition of shipment/sell-in/sell-through/activation;
- same storage/memory tier for price comparison;
- same camera mode/test condition for performance evidence.

When numbers are not comparable, use a qualitative table instead of a bar chart.

## Market/user evidence

Do not infer age, gender, income, or motivations from product design alone. When direct survey evidence is unavailable, build **need-state/user-job hypotheses** and label them inference.

## Platform research

For Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Bilibili, TikTok, Shopee, Amazon, etc. distinguish:

- publicly verifiable signals;
- logged-in platform data;
- brand-owned backend data;
- creator-side analytics;
- anecdotal screenshots.

Never fabricate search volume, “viral post count”, creator conversion, GMV, CTR, or CVR.

## Citations in the HTML

The report needs a visible Sources & Reliability appendix. Each source entry should include:

- A/B/C grade;
- title/organization;
- direct URL when available;
- what it supports;
- date or access snapshot when time-sensitive.

For important numbers, put the source/caveat next to the chart as well as in the appendix.
