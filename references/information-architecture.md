# Default information architecture

Use this order unless the business question clearly requires a change.

## Hero

- report title and product/competitor;
- market;
- analysis date;
- goal;
- direct pairing snapshot.

## 1. Executive Summary

4–6 decisive conclusions, followed by one recommended positioning statement. Show the most decision-useful metrics, not the largest number of metrics.

## 2. Competitor Scope

Direct / indirect / reference competitors and why. Clarify unavailable/unreleased SKUs.

## 3. Market Context

Only market facts that change the GTM decision: category growth/decline, premiumization, pricing pressure, channel shift, regulation, replacement cycle, etc.

## 4. User + Positioning

Need-state segments, use cases, payer/influencer if relevant, decision pain points, and the mental choice each brand wants to own.

## 5. Product + Selling Points

Equivalent-SKU comparison table and “so what?” column. Separate spec facts from experiential claims.

## 6. Pricing Architecture

Compare like-for-like storage/configuration. Show entry price, key price anchors, bundles, financing, trade-in, subscription/service, and ownership cost when relevant.

## 7. Brand Message / Mental Territory

Show official messaging, evidence behind it, and where the two brands overlap or differ.

## 8. Channel + Content

Platform role, content archetype, creator role, search questions, commerce handoff, and what must be verified in first-party data.

## 9. Full Conversion Journey

Use the journey strip plus a table:

`stage | competitor win reason | our counteraction | metric`

## 10. Early Market / User Signals

Only when reliable enough. Label third-party or directional data clearly.

## 11. Opportunities and Risks

Four-card structure works well:

- competitor did right;
- our existing asset;
- our risk;
- competitor vulnerability.

## 12. Positioning Recommendation

Three-level ladder:

1. core mental territory;
2. rational proof;
3. scene-based hooks.

Then give product/SKU-specific messaging.

## 13. GTM Operating Actions

Use a priority table:

`P0/P1/P2 | strategic move | concrete execution | KPI`

Cover content, search, creator, commerce/PDP, pricing/promo, paid media, retail/trial, CRM/service as relevant.

## 14. Test Plan

At least 4–6 tests. Each test needs:

`single variable | A vs B | target audience/channel | primary outcome | guardrail metric`

## 15. One-page Conclusion

Compress the full report to a single causal chain and a small set of product-specific rules.

## 16. Sources & Reliability

Graded source list + research boundaries + unsupported-data gaps that require first-party validation.
